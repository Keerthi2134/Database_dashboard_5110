Analytical Dashboard: OLAP Demonstration Prompts
Presentation–Time OLAP Narratives
E1. GC leaderboard with contextual descent.Begin from the overall General Classifica-
tion, ordered by the lowest cumulative∑adjusted_time. Follow a chosen contender down
to the stage grain to surface the single day that most altered their GC standing, framing
it as a pivotal moment in their race.
E2. A stage in focus.Treat one stage as a vignette: present the leading finishers with their
adjusted times, riders, teams, origins and destinations, distance, and stage type. Reframe
the view to a team perspective to reveal which squads populated the top ten on that day.
E3. Team burden and contribution.Roll up each team’s aggregate adjusted time over the
event, then open the aggregation to reveal which riders carried the time load and how their
per-stage profiles shaped the team’s outcome.
E4. Parallel classifications.Place side-by-side a rider’s GC rank and their Young Rider GC
rank (with an age filter anchored at race start). Introduce amountain-stage GC proxy
by accumulating adjusted time only on “Mountain” stages, and narrate where the proxy
diverges from overall GC.
E5. Mountain contours.For riders, surface two mountain-specific signals: podium frequency
on Mountain stages and mean time gap to the stage winner on that terrain; allow a return
to the daily traces that produced these summaries.
E6. Speed as a simple ratio.For riders and teams, chart a stage-type profile using the
proxy length/time across Flat, Hilly, Mountain, and ITT. Emphasize who appears to gain
when the terrain shifts away from Flat/Hilly toward Mountain.
E7. Bonuses, penalties, and counterfactuals.Lay out cumulative bonuses and penalties
per rider; then replay the GC leaderboard under counterfactual toggles (first removing
bonuses, then penalties) to illuminate movements into and out of the top ten.
E8. Participation by nation.Summarize nations by their presence—count of teams and
count of riders—then open the roll-up to list the riders and their associated teams for a
chosen country.
E9. National outcomes (within available data).For each nation, note the best GC rank
achieved by its riders and tally wins/podiums; keep the pathway back to the underlying
stages and athletes clear.
E10. Temporal GC storyline.Trace cumulative adjusted time after each stage for the prin-
cipal GC actors as a line narrative; mark Mountain stages and annotate inflection points
where standings reshuffle, with rider/team filters guiding the lens.
E11. Against the clock.Within the time-trial context, surface the inventory of ITT stages
and present their essentials—date, distance, and winner—as a compact catalogue.

most often appear in the top ten; accompany the summary with the list of contributing

E13. Depth of squads.At the rider grain, portray team depth via distinct rider counts,
highlighting squads with the broadest rosters and providing a doorway to their full rider
lists and countries.
E14. Top-ten intensity by team.Aggregate top-ten presences at the team level and, where
helpful, break them down by stage type; pair the totals with links back to the specific
stages and riders involved.
